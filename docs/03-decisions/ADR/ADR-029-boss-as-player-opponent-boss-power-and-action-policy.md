# ADR-029: Boss as a Genuine Opponent — Boss Power, Boss Cards, and the Response-Phase Action Policy

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-18
**Deciders:** Product Owner (operator), Systems Designer, Backend Architecture, Client Architecture
**Consulted:** AGENTS.md, docs/AGENTS.md, MVP_SCOPE.md, GDD.md, GAME_RULES.md, BOSS_RULES.md, CARD_RULES.md, COMBAT_RULES.md, GAME_STATE.md, GAME_EVENTS.md, SIGNALR_PROTOCOL.md, REDIS_STATE.md, ARCHITECTURE.md, ADR-001, ADR-008, ADR-021, ADR-024, ADR-026, ADR-028
**Supersedes:** `TASK-258` §2 **D2** (the decision that forbade `BossState.Power`), and the `POWER` bullet of `ADR-026` Decision item 4 / its Unresolved Balance Choices #1–#2 (partially — see the Supersession Record below)

## 1. Context

### 1.1 The system this decision changes

In the in-force design, the Boss is a board-acting combatant whose abilities are
gated by a private counter. `ADR-026` gave both actors one shared 8×8 board and
actor-relative match effects; it kept the Boss's ability resource as
`BossState.SkillCharge`, and the `POWER` Gem's Boss-side output routed into that
counter rather than into a Power resource. `TASK-258` §2 **D2** fixed that split
as an explicit Product Owner decision:

> "Retain `BossState.SkillCharge` as the single Boss ability resource. Do not
> introduce `BossState.Power`. … Player POWER gem matches must not charge the
> Boss. Specific charge units and threshold values remain balance-tuning
> decisions."

That decision is transcribed in `BOSS_RULES.md` §6.3, `COMBAT_RULES.md` §2,
`GAME_RULES.md` §17 step 18a, `GAME_STATE.md` §2.4.3 and `ADR-026` Decision item 4.

### 1.2 Verified implementation facts at the decision point

These were read from source at the decision point, and they are recorded here
because the migration's scope depends on them. **They are findings, not design
proposals.**

1. **Pet Power and Boss charge are generated by different rules today.**
   `src/backend/GameServer.Domain/Match3/ResourceGenerator.cs`
   `ApplyActorMatchEffects` has two branches. The Player branch calls
   `ApplyPower`, which adds the generated POWER pool (`BaseOutput(POWER) ×
   uniquely matched POWER cells`, currently `10` per Gem) and clamps to `0–100`.
   The Boss branch calls `ApplyBossSkillCharge`, which adds
   `CountPowerMatchGroups(resolution)` — **+1 per POWER match group** — and
   clamps at the Boss's `ChargeRequirement`, not at `100`.
2. **The Boss has no Power member.**
   `src/backend/GameServer.Domain/Battle/BossState.cs` is a record struct with
   eleven positional members (`BossId`, `Element`, `HP`, `MaxHP`, `ATK`, `DEF`,
   `State`, `PassiveId`, `PassiveProgress`, `SkillCharge`, `SkillCooldown`) plus
   `ActiveStatusEffects`, and its `Equals`/`GetHashCode` enumerate exactly those.
3. **The Card executor is Player-anchored.**
   `src/backend/GameServer.Domain/Cards/CardCastExecutor.cs` exposes
   `Execute(BattleState state, CardDefinition cardDefinition)` with no actor
   parameter, validates against `state.PetState`'s loadout/Power/allowance,
   rejects any category other than `Basic`/`PetSkill` (`"InvalidCard"`), and
   `MaxCardCastsPerTurn = 1`. `CardCategory` has exactly `Basic` and `PetSkill`.
   There is no `BossCardExecutor` anywhere in the repository.
4. **The Boss response is sequenced in the Application layer.**
   `GameServer.Application/Battle/BattleStateService.cs` runs the step-18 phase:
   its Boss branch calls `ApplyActorMatchEffects(DamageParty.Boss, …)` (comment:
   "charge BossState.SkillCharge, +1 per match group and clamped to …"), then
   evaluates `SkillCharge >= SkillChargeRequirement` and fires the Skill.
5. **Scope and client-visibility constraints were closed.** `MVP_SCOPE.md` §1
   listed no Boss Power and no Boss Card category (§3 listed "More Cards /
   Relics" as FUTURE; §4 defaults unlisted content to FUTURE);
   `SIGNALR_PROTOCOL.md` §4.4 item 3 enumerated `SkillCharge`/`SkillCooldown`
   among the members that may not be delivered without their own protocol change;
   `GAME_RULES.md` §16's event list and `SIGNALR_PROTOCOL.md` §3.2.2's
   discriminator set contained no Boss Card event.

**Consequence recorded explicitly: the Boss's resource generation is *not*
already equivalent to the Pet's.** It differs in unit metric (+1 per match group
vs. `BaseOutput` per Gem), in clamp target (`ChargeRequirement` vs. `0–100`), and
in storage (`SkillCharge` vs. `Power`). No claim that the two are already
equivalent may be made, and the migration must change all three.

### 1.3 The Product Owner decision this ADR records

The Product Owner decided that **the Boss must behave as a genuine opponent using
the same resource and cast principles as the Player**, rather than remaining a
`SkillCharge`-driven actor, and authorized the following:

1. Reverse `TASK-258` §2 D2 explicitly: introduce `BossState.Power`, constrained
   to integer 0–100, into MVP scope.
2. Use the same resource-generation and cost-validation rules for Player and Boss
   wherever the game rules define them as shared; preserve actor-specific effects
   where the rules require them. **No audit-derived proposal is approved** — in
   particular the "+10 Power per gem" conversion and any HP / Pet-Power threshold
   are not adopted.
3. Preserve separate `PetState` and `BossState`; share appropriate cast
   contracts, validation and effect services; do not merge the actor states.
4. Relics remain Player-only.
5. Each Boss response phase follows an explicit AI action policy that chooses
   **either** a board match action **or** a Card cast, rather than automatically
   casting every eligible ability; determinism, eligibility, action ordering,
   cooldown handling and tie-break behavior are defined in authoritative
   documentation **before** runtime implementation.
6. Encounter mechanics (Enrage, HP-threshold healing, combo-triggered passives)
   remain passive or trigger-based mechanics, not player-like Card actions.
7. Introduce a dedicated Boss action event; preserve the existing Player
   `CardCast` wire contract.
8. Move Boss Power and the minimum required Boss Card capability into MVP; keep
   the fifth Gem outside MVP until the migration's Slices 1–4 are stable.

No feasibility report was supplied or present in the repository, so no
audit-derived value is treated as evidence anywhere in this record.

### 1.4 Why an ADR is required

The change touches the authoritative battle-state model (a new `BossState`
member), the realtime contract (a new event discriminator and a widened
`bossState` projection), the MVP scope classification (FUTURE → IN), the
resolution order's Boss phase, the module boundary for Card execution, and a
standing Product Owner decision. `AGENTS.md` §18 and
`.ai/workflow/architecture/adr-change.md` §1 require an ADR before any such change
is implemented, and `docs/03-decisions/README.md` §3 requires the underlying
rules to be owned by the game-design and technical documents rather than by this
record.

## 2. Decision

### 2.1 Boss Power (reverses TASK-258 §2 D2)

1. **`BossState.Power` is introduced into MVP scope** as the Boss's own battle
   resource: an integer in the range **0–100**, clamped at both ends, `0` at
   battle creation, never nullable and never omitted.
2. **`TASK-258` §2 D2 is superseded.** The rule "do not introduce
   `BossState.Power`" no longer holds. Its other clauses are preserved: Boss Power
   is generated **only** from POWER Gems the Boss itself matched, and Player POWER
   matches must not generate Boss Power. The decision's "specific charge units and
   threshold values remain balance-tuning decisions" clause carries forward as
   this record's unresolved balance items (§3).
3. **The state rule is not owned here.** The member's shape, initial value,
   write points and lifecycle are `GAME_STATE.md` §2.4/§2.4.3's; the gameplay
   rules are `BOSS_RULES.md` §4.1's; the generation rate table is
   `COMBAT_RULES.md` §2's; the general Power rules are `GAME_RULES.md` §12's.

### 2.2 One shared resource-generation rule, actor-relative

4. **Player and Boss use the same generation rule wherever the rules define it as
   shared.** POWER Gems are the Power-generating Gem type for both actors, and the
   contribution rule is `COMBAT_RULES.md` §2 item 6's (`BaseOutput(type) ×
   unique matched count(type)`, accumulated per pass) applied to whichever actor
   generated the match. There is no Boss-specific generation *formula* once
   Slice 2 lands.
5. **Actor-specific effects are preserved where the rules require them.** DEF
   Gems, HP Gems, ATK gating and every actor-relative effect stay exactly as
   `ADR-026` item 4 leaves them (as amended by `ADR-026`'s `TASK-299` amendment for
   DEF). This decision changes the POWER output's **destination**, not the other
   three Gem types.
6. **No numeric value is authored.** This decision authors no rate, no cost, no
   threshold and no magnitude. The shared `BaseOutput` value is the one
   `COMBAT_RULES.md` §2 already holds for the Player and is not changed here; the
   audit-derived conversion proposal and every threshold from it are **not
   adopted**, and the audit's arithmetic must not be reconstructed as a rule
   (§3, U-1/U-2).
7. **Cost validation is shared.** A Boss Card cast composes and validates its
   `EffectiveCardCost` through the same contract as a Player cast
   (`CARD_RULES.md` §3.6), with an empty modifier set because Relics are
   Player-only, and deducts from `BossState.Power`.

### 2.3 Separate actor states, shared cast services

8. **`PetState` and `BossState` remain separate.** They are not merged, not
   unified into a common "actor state", and neither is projected as the other.
   The shared surface is the **cast contract, validation and effect services** —
   not the state.
9. **The shared cast contract is `CARD_RULES.md` §3's**, extended by its item 9 to
   state the actor-relative split: shared validation order, shared cost
   composition, shared effect resolution; Player-only allowance, Player-only Relic
   triggers and Player-only Relic cost modifiers.
10. **Ownership stays inside the Boss module for Boss behaviour.** The
    response-phase action selection is a Boss concern (`BOSS_RULES.md` §4.3), the
    ability set is Boss content (§4.2), and the event is the Boss's (§4.4, §2.6
    below). The Card module owns what a Card *is* and how a cast resolves; it does
    not decide what the Boss does.

### 2.4 Relics remain Player-only

11. A Relic never reads or writes `BossState.Power`, never triggers on a Boss Card
    cast, and never modifies a Boss Card's cost. `PetState.CardCostModifiers[]`
    stays the Pet-side Relic carrier; the Boss Card's modifier set is empty by
    construction, not by convention.

### 2.5 Boss Card category and the minimum MVP capability

12. **A `Boss Card` category is added** to `CARD_RULES.md` §1 as the Boss's own
    Card category, and the **minimum required capability** moves into MVP: each of
    the five MVP Bosses carries exactly **one** authored Boss Card, carrying that
    Boss's MVP ability.
13. **No Boss loadout, no Boss Card collection, no ownership row, no acquisition
    path, and no Player visibility.** The Player's loadout, ownership and
    collection rules are unchanged.
14. **The ability's identity is reused, not duplicated.** A Boss Card's
    `CardDefinitionId` is the ability identity already recorded in
    `BOSS_RULES.md` §6.4's `SkillId` column, so no second identifier exists for
    the same ability.
15. **Persistence is out of this decision's scope.** No database column, table,
    migration or provisioning is authored; provisioning Boss Cards is a separate
    future task.

### 2.6 Encounter mechanics stay passive

16. **Enrage, HP-threshold healing, and HP-threshold or Combo-threshold passives
    are not Boss Cards and never become Boss Cards.** They carry no Power cost, no
    cast, and no cast event, and they resolve at the points their own sections fix.
    Architectural symmetry is **not** a reason to convert them, and this record
    forbids doing so.

### 2.7 Response-phase action policy (one action, explicit and deterministic)

17. **The Boss takes exactly one action per response phase:** either a board match
    action or a Boss Card cast. It never performs both, and it never automatically
    casts every eligible ability.
18. **The policy is defined in `BOSS_RULES.md` §4.3 before any runtime
    implementation**, covering all five required aspects:

    ```text
    determinism      pure function of the committed state + Boss definition;
                     zero unseeded PRNG, no wall clock, no client input; fixed
                     candidate enumeration order; same state → same action
    eligibility      hard gates: board action available iff the legal-move
                     enumeration finds a match-producing swap (deadlock recovery
                     unchanged); cast available iff the Boss Card exists, Boss
                     Power >= EffectiveCardCost, and cooldown = 0
    action ordering  selection at 18a; board action (if selected) at 18a;
                     Passive at 18b; cast (if selected) at 18c; attack check at
                     18d — the step list is unchanged and only one of 18a/18c
                     does work
    cooldown         hard eligibility gate; −1 per Turn increment; set to the
                     authored value on a successful cast; keeps running during
                     board-action phases
    tie-break        a tie resolves in favour of the BOARD MATCH ACTION — a cast
                     must strictly out-score the board action; board-action ties
                     keep ADR-026 item 8's lowest cell-index rule
    ```

19. **A cast phase's consequences are documented, not hidden.** Because a cast
    phase has no Boss board resolution, the Boss generates no board resources and
    no Boss attack occurs that phase (the attack is gated on a confirmed ATK match
    in the Boss's own resolution, `ADR-026` D1). That is a deliberate consequence
    of the one-action policy, and its pacing effect is an unresolved balance item
    (§3, U-3).
20. **The cast-priority score's authored form and values are unresolved.** The
    gates, ordering, tie-break and determinism are decided; the Boss-specific
    selection weights are content/balance values (§3, U-3), and Slice 3 may not be
    implemented before they are approved.

### 2.8 A dedicated Boss action event; the Player `CardCast` contract is preserved

21. **`BossCardCast` is added** as a new canonical event name (`GAME_RULES.md`
    §16), a new discriminator and wire schema (`SIGNALR_PROTOCOL.md` §3.2.2,
    §3.2.30), and a new event meaning/payload (`GAME_EVENTS.md` §2).
22. **The Player `CardCast` wire contract is preserved exactly.** It keeps
    `type` + `cardId` only; it gains no actor/source member and no cost member; it
    is never emitted for a Boss cast. The discriminator is the distinction, so no
    consumer migration of the existing payload is required.
23. **`BossSkillCast` remains the transitional path's event** until the removal
    gate in §2.9 is met, and no new work may build on it.

### 2.9 Staged migration and the `SkillCharge` removal gate

24. **The migration is staged into four slices** (§4). Runtime implementation is
    gated behind them, and **no slice may start before its prerequisites are
    approved**.
25. **`SkillCharge` and the legacy handlers are removed only after parity has been
    demonstrated by tests and all consumers have been reconciled.** The gate is:
    (a) all five abilities migrated and covered by tests; (b) old and new
    behavior compared, including the **exact qualifying attack** and the
    **modifier-consumption timing** of the trigger-based ability; (c) every
    consumer reconciled — event presenters, wire tests, state readers, and
    documentation; (d) the removal's ADR/index amendments recorded.
26. **Until the gate is met, both the target contract and the transitional path
    are documented, and no third variant is permitted.** The target contract is
    authoritative for new work; `SkillCharge`, the charge requirement and
    `BossSkillCast` are a compatibility path being retired, never a second live
    design.
27. **This task implements no code.** No runtime code, database schema, test code
    or protocol implementation changes are authorized by this decision; the
    documents were amended first and this ADR is the historical record of the
    decision, per `docs/03-decisions/README.md` §1 and
    `.ai/workflow/architecture/adr-change.md` §2.

### 2.10 MVP scope move and the fifth Gem

28. **Boss Power and the minimum Boss Card capability move from FUTURE to IN** in
    `MVP_SCOPE.md` §1–§3, made on this recorded Product Owner approval through the
    `GAME_RULES.md` §20 Rule Change process.
29. **The fifth Gem type stays outside MVP.** It is not authorized, designed,
    provisioned or scaffolded, and it remains gated until Slices 1–4 are stable
    and its own task plus its own rule change exists (`MVP_SCOPE.md` §3).

### 2.11 Boss Power projection and reconnect

30. **`bossState` gains an always-present `power` member** (`SIGNALR_PROTOCOL.md`
    §4.4 item 12): the Boss's integer 0–100 resource, read directly from
    `BossState.Power`, delivered on the existing `BattleStateUpdated` push and
    therefore on the existing §7 reconnect snapshot.
31. **No new delivery path exists for it.** There is no `BossPowerChanged` event,
    no new hub method, no subscription, and no second projection. Reconnect is
    snapshot resynchronization (`ADR-008`), unchanged.
32. **It is not `SkillCharge`.** `SkillCharge` and `SkillCooldown` stay
    server-side; the two values are never conflated on the wire, and no consumer
    may reconstruct one from the other.

## 4. Staged Implementation Plan (Documented, Not Executed)

Each slice is a separate future task with its own declared file set and review
gate. No slice is authorized to start by this record; each requires the
unresolved values it depends on to be approved first (§3).

```text
SLICE 1 — Boss Power foundation
  Boss Power generation (grant basis U-1 approved first), 0–100 clamping, state
  serialization, Redis round trip, state projection (bossState.power), HUD
  visibility, and reconnect recovery.
  Inspect-first requirement: the existing ResourceGenerator and actor-relative
  effect behaviour were inspected before this scope was estimated (§1.2) — the
  Boss branch currently charges SkillCharge +1 per match group clamped at
  ChargeRequirement, so Slice 1 must ADD a boss Power path beside it, not reuse it.
  Exit: a Boss Power value is generated, clamped, persisted, projected, rendered
  and recovered across a reconnect; SkillCharge is untouched and still in force.

SLICE 2 — Shared cast contracts and effects
  Shared cast contract, actor/target resolution, cost validation, self/opponent
  effects, and Player Card regression coverage.
  Exit: one cast contract serves both actors; the Player's CardCast behavior,
  its one-per-Turn allowance, its Relic triggers and its cost modifiers are
  unchanged and covered by regression tests; a Boss cast can be validated and
  resolved against BossState.Power with the same machinery.

SLICE 3 — Deterministic Boss AI action policy
  The one-action policy (BOSS_RULES.md §4.3): eligibility, deterministic
  selection, cooldown handling, board-action ordering, tie-break, and the
  dedicated Boss event's presentation (BossCardCast).
  Exit: the Boss takes exactly one action per response phase, deterministically;
  a cast phase produces no Boss board resolution and no Boss attack; both action
  kinds are presented from their own events.

SLICE 4 — Ability migration
  Migrate Earthquake, then Flame Burst, then Root, then Drain Power; migrate
  Thunder Strike LAST.
  Per-ability comparison requirement: compare old and new behavior, especially
  the exact qualifying attack and the modifier-consumption timing (Thunder
  Strike's NextQualifyingAttack expiry is the reason it goes last).
  Exit: each ability resolves as a Boss Card with the same authored effect,
  target and duration, with costs and any magnitudes approved (U-2, U-4, U-5).

SLICE 5 — Removal (gated, not scheduled)
  Remove SkillCharge and the legacy handlers only after §2.9's four-part parity
  gate is met and every consumer is reconciled.
```

Ordering constraints that are not negotiable: Slice 2 before Slice 4 (an ability
cannot migrate without the shared contract); Slice 3 before Slice 4 (an ability
cannot be selected by a policy that does not exist); Slice 1 before Slice 3 (the
policy's cast candidate needs Power); Slice 5 last, only on demonstrated parity.

## 5. Alternatives Considered

- **Keep `SkillCharge` only (the status quo, reaffirming `TASK-258` §2 D2).**
  Rejected by the Product Owner: it leaves the Boss driven by a private counter
  rather than the same resource principles as the Player, and the "genuine
  opponent" requirement cannot be met without a Power economy.
- **Replace `SkillCharge` with `Power` in one step, including the ability
  migration.** Rejected: it would change the resource, the cast gate, five
  ability effects, the wire contract and the state model simultaneously, with no
  parity evidence and no way to compare old and new behavior — the reason this
  record is a staged migration rather than a single change.
- **Merge `PetState` and `BossState` into one actor-relative state.** Rejected by
  the Product Owner's third decision item, and independently undesirable: the two
  actors carry different fields (loadout, Passive progress, Relic carriers vs.
  Boss ability state, Enrage), and a merged state would either grow both or force
  absent-field conventions the state model deliberately avoids
  (`GAME_STATE.md` §0 item 5).
- **Keep the current "board action, then cast if eligible" ordering.** Rejected:
  that *is* the behaviour the Product Owner reversed — the Boss acting on the
  board **and** casting in the same response phase is not a choice. The
  one-action policy is the requirement, and the documented consequence (no attack
  in a cast phase) is accepted rather than special-cased away.
- **Put the Boss's decision inside a generic multi-actor action framework.**
  Rejected: no second consumer exists, and `AGENTS.md` §9 (and
  `ARCHITECTURE.md` §5) forbid inventing an abstraction before it is required.
  The Boss's policy is a Boss-module responsibility and the Card contract is
  shared; neither needs a framework.
- **Extend `CardCast` with an actor/source member instead of adding an event.**
  Rejected: it would edit a pinned Player-facing payload for every consumer to
  distinguish one case, while a dedicated discriminator is additive, tolerated by
  the existing tolerant-reader rule (`SIGNALR_PROTOCOL.md` §3.2.2 item 6), and
  leaves the Player contract byte-identical.
- **Turn encounter mechanics (Enrage, HP-threshold regeneration, combo Rage) into
  Boss Cards for symmetry.** Rejected explicitly by the Product Owner's sixth
  decision item and forbidden by `BOSS_RULES.md` §4.2 item 7: they are
  passive/trigger-based, and symmetry is not a design reason.
- **Answer the balance questions now from the audit's proposals.** Rejected: the
  cited feasibility audit is absent from the repository, its values are unverified
  proposals, and `AGENTS.md` §7/§20 forbid promoting a proposal to an
  authoritative rule. They are recorded as unresolved instead (§3).

## 6. Consequences

### Positive

- **The Boss becomes an opponent, not a target.** It banks and spends the same
  resource the Player does, and its decision to act or cast is an explicit,
  reviewable policy rather than an emergent side effect.
- **One resource rule instead of two.** The POWER Gem's Boss-side behaviour stops
  being a private charge metric, which removes the standing divergence between
  the documented "unresolved charge metric" and the runtime's `+1 per match
  group` adoption.
- **The Player's contracts are untouched.** `CardCast`, the one-cast-per-Turn
  allowance, the Relic system and the ownership model are unchanged, so the
  Player-side regression surface is bounded on purpose.
- **Additive wire and state changes.** One event discriminator plus one
  always-present projection member, both tolerated by the existing reader rules;
  no version negotiation, no protocol rewrite.
- **Retirement has an evidence gate.** `SkillCharge` is not deleted on a promise:
  parity must be demonstrated by tests and consumers must be reconciled first.

### Negative / Trade-offs

- **A documented target-state divergence exists until Slices 1–4 land.** The
  authoritative documents describe the migration's end state while the runtime
  still executes the legacy path for unmigrated abilities. That is deliberate —
  documentation-first (`AGENTS.md` §17, `ADR-028`'s precedent) — but it means the
  divergence must stay explicitly recorded, and no slice may silently leave it
  stale.
- **Two Boss resources coexist for the migration window.** `Power` and
  `SkillCharge` are both present and must never be converted into one another;
  the transitional window is a real complexity cost, accepted so parity can be
  measured.
- **Balance risk is concentrated and deferred.** A shared per-Gem rate makes the
  Boss's economy much faster than `+1 per match group`, and a cast phase forgoes
  the Boss's attack. Both are balance questions this record refuses to answer
  (§3, U-1/U-3), which means Slice 1 and Slice 3 cannot complete without explicit
  operator decisions.
- **The Boss's cast is a new presentation surface.** A new event needs frontend
  presentation, and the ability's identity is now reported by two event shapes
  during the migration window (legacy and Card), which consumers must handle
  without conflating them.
- **Scope grew.** MVP now includes a Boss resource and a Boss Card capability,
  which is more content surface than the pre-decision scope.
